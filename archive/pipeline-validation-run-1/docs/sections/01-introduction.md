Fouling progressively adds thermal resistance to heat-transfer surfaces, degrading heat-transfer performance and increasing the energy required to maintain duty. In shell-and-tube exchangers used across power and process industries, this degradation motivates models that can estimate fouling resistance accurately enough to inform the timing of inspection and cleaning. Mechanistic work has represented crude-oil fouling in dynamic, distributed shell-and-tube models and in subsequent reactive-deposit models of fouling–cleaning cycles (@coletti2011dynamic; @dazbejarano2016new). These studies establish the practical modeling context, but the present work does not attempt to reproduce their industrial validation. Instead, it asks a narrower methodological question about surrogate behavior when operating conditions fall outside the training envelope.

Machine learning offers fast surrogates for fouling-related quantities. Recent studies have trained data-driven networks on CFD-generated shell-and-tube fouling data (@he2025machine) and compared regression families for estimating heat-exchanger fouling factors (@hosseini2022novel). Because these two sources were available here only at abstract level, the absence of an extrapolation design or an embedded physics correlation is not claimed for their full texts; rather, neither feature is reported in the abstracts reviewed. Physics can also enter learning in materially different ways. For example, physics-informed monitoring of a rotary air preheater has enforced governing heat-transfer equations through loss penalties and then calculated fouling propensity from the predicted thermal field (@jadhav2022physics). More generally, first-principles and data-driven components can be combined through hybrid submodeling, including additive structures, rather than through a physics-penalized loss alone (@bradley2022perspectives). These designs test different estimands and should not be treated as interchangeable.

The most direct precedent is @gallup2023physics, which compared a black-box surrogate, physics-guided loss formulations, and a residual-type hybrid under an explicit out-of-training-range test for a continuous stirred-tank reactor. Its evidence is partly negative for the architecture considered here: the residual hybrid was only slightly ahead of the black-box baseline, whereas the loss-penalty PINN was far ahead of both. At the same time, the residual hybrid used 5-node hidden layers, while the baseline used six layers of 20, and the study reported no paired repeated-seed test. The comparison therefore confounds trainable capacity with architectural effect; the direction of that confounding is not presumed. On the searches conducted through 2026-09, no matched-capacity comparison was found that decides whether an additive-residual physics-informed hybrid outperforms a black-box outside the training envelope, and no such comparison addressed shell-and-tube fouling resistance. This is a search-qualified gap, not a universal absence claim; it also leaves the stronger loss-penalty result in @gallup2023physics intact.

Accordingly, the exact study question is: **For shell-and-tube heat exchanger fouling resistance simulated by a mechanistic fouling model, does embedding a physics-based fouling correlation inside a machine-learning surrogate lower RMSE on operating conditions held outside the training envelope, compared to a capacity-matched purely data-driven model?** The intended contribution is a controlled synthetic benchmark: an additive-residual hybrid and a black-box network will be matched in trainable capacity, given identical hyperparameter-search budgets, evaluated over repeated paired seeds, and compared with a preregistered paired test. This design is meant to isolate the comparison more cleanly than the direct precedent. It is not a claim that additive-residual models are generally superior to black-box models, loss-penalty PINNs, or other hybrid families.

Four preregistered hypotheses and design contingencies govern the test; none is treated here as an experimental finding. C0 requires the embedded physics component to be an independently selected published Ebert–Panchal correlation, documented in @wilson2017twenty, while the synthetic simulator contains an omitted induction mechanism motivated independently by @yang2020computational. This construction is intended to give the learned residual a genuine discrepancy to model, but that workload remains to be demonstrated. C0 adjudication will first be suspended, leaving C0 `assumed`, if the median residual-free correlation extrapolation RMSE across the ten paired repetitions exceeds the black-box median extrapolation RMSE, because that pattern triggers a parameter-transcription, unit-conversion, or input-mapping alarm. Otherwise, C0 will be refuted if the residual-free median extrapolation RMSE, scored against noisy held-out targets, is at or below 1.1 times the added-noise standard deviation; only passage through these preregistered gates can support the premise that the residual had real work to do.

C1 hypothesizes that extrapolation is genuinely harder for the black-box: across ten repetitions, its median extrapolation RMSE must be at least 1.5 times its median interpolation RMSE. C2 hypothesizes an extrapolation advantage for the matched hybrid, requiring at least a 10% reduction in median RMSE, lower error in at least eight of ten paired repetitions, and a one-sided exact Wilcoxon signed-rank result with p < 0.05. C3 hypothesizes that this potential extrapolation benefit does not come at a material interpolation cost, defined as hybrid median interpolation RMSE no more than 10% above the black-box median. Detailed splits, equations, seed pairing, and adjudication rules are reserved for Methods. If C1 fails, C2 and C3 will be reported as uninterpretable rather than supported or refuted; if C0 is refuted or its adjudication is suspended and remains `assumed`, C2 and C3 cannot be reported as supported.

The scope is deliberately limited to a controlled synthetic tube-side benchmark with fixed fluid properties and fixed wall temperature. It is not a validated model of a complete shell-and-tube exchanger, an industrial digital twin, or a basis for cleaning decisions. Any eventual inference must therefore remain conditional on the selected correlation, omitted induction mechanism, synthetic operating domain, capacity controls, and preregistered evaluation design.
